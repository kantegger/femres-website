Loretta J. Ross's article is a core entry point for understanding reproductive justice. It returns the framework to the history of women of color organizing, Black feminism, and human rights politics instead of reducing it to a more inclusive language for abortion rights. Ross emphasizes that reproductive freedom includes the right not to have children, the right to have children, and the right to raise children in safe and resourced communities.

The article matters because it connects bodily autonomy with poverty, racism, immigration control, environmental violence, medical systems, and state punishment. For many marginalized people, legal choice is not the same as actual capacity. Reproductive justice therefore demands changes in resource distribution, community safety, health care access, and political representation.

For FemRes, this article can serve as a theoretical anchor for the reproductive justice topic. It helps readers distinguish rights, health, and justice frameworks, and it reminds a feminist archive to preserve not only legal disputes but also movement thinking about freedom, family, and futures.
