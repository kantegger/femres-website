On “Feminist Wellness,” Béa Victoria Albina examines what she calls emotional outsourcing: the learned habit of looking to other people for permission, safety, validation, and proof of worth. Episodes connect people-pleasing, perfectionism, codependent patterns, functional freeze, and difficulty resting to the body’s adaptations under gendered and familial expectations. The show combines coaching, nursing experience, somatic practices, and popular explanations of nervous-system regulation, usually ending with a concrete exercise or question listeners can try in everyday relationships.

Its feminist contribution is to refuse the idea that boundaries are simply a matter of stronger personality. Many women and feminized people are rewarded for anticipating others, avoiding conflict, and making their own needs invisible; those strategies may once have protected attachment or safety. Albina asks listeners to approach them with curiosity rather than shame, then practice tolerating the discomfort of acting differently. This can make “self-care” less about consumption and more about recovering agency over attention, time, bodily signals, and consent.

The podcast sits at the border of feminist education, wellness culture, and commercial coaching, so critical listening matters. Neuroscience language can sound more settled or universal than the evidence warrants, and somatic exercises are not a replacement for medical care, psychotherapy, material security, or political action. The show is strongest when its tools are treated as experiments rather than prescriptions and when personal change remains connected to the conditions that trained self-abandonment in the first place. For FemRes readers, it is useful both as a practical resource and as a case study in how feminist ideas are translated into contemporary therapeutic culture.
