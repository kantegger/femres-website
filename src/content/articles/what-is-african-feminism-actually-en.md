Minna Salami approaches definition as a political task rather than a search for one pure formula. African feminism develops through anti-colonial struggle, women’s organizing, literature, digital networks, legal reform, and arguments among different generations. It cannot be reduced to a local copy of Euro-American feminism, but neither is it sealed off from global exchange.

The essay is especially useful for identifying the double bind imposed on African feminists. From outside, Africa is treated as a site where Western theory arrives to save women; from within nationalist or religious politics, feminism is dismissed as foreign. Salami contests both stories by foregrounding African women’s long histories of political action and their active production of theory.

Her map is necessarily selective, and readers should use it as an entrance rather than a canon. The growth of queer, disability, labor, and digital feminist work continues to change the field. What remains stable is the method: concepts must be rooted in African histories and power relations while remaining open to solidarity and critique across borders.
