Sisonke Msimang begins as someone who believes in stories, then identifies the limits of that belief. Personal narratives can interrupt stereotype and make distant lives emotionally legible, but audiences may confuse feeling with participation. A moving testimony becomes content consumed, praised, and forgotten while the institution producing the injustice remains intact.

Her argument is not a rejection of art or lived experience. It is a demand that stories remain connected to facts, history, and organized action. The speaker’s vulnerability should not carry the entire burden of proving injustice, and empathy should not allow the listener to occupy the flattering role of a good person without accepting responsibility.

For feminist media practice, the talk offers a valuable test: after representation, what changes? Msimang asks readers and viewers to move from identification toward plans, institutions, and solidarity. Stories can open political attention, but only collective action can keep that opening from becoming another brief emotional event.
