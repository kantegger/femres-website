Tashny Sukumaran begins from a break between Malaysian women’s strong university performance and their labor-force participation. Education systems can produce large cohorts of female graduates while labor markets and household institutions fail to let them remain employed. Ability has not suddenly disappeared; responsibility for children, older relatives, and household coordination concentrates on women at decisive career stages.

The talk places office and home within one analytical frame. Unaffordable childcare, long hours, rewards for constant availability, motherhood penalties, and the assumption of men as primary earners combine into pressure to leave. A company that offers women leadership training without changing its time regime and care support may simply teach individuals to adapt to a structure that continues excluding caregivers.

This TEDx talk is public advocacy rather than a complete account of class, migration, informal work, or policy cost. It nevertheless makes the missing connection clear: educational achievement does not create an automatic path to economic equality. Public childcare, shared household responsibility, predictable hours, paid care leave, and promotion systems that do not punish caregivers are needed—not another demand that women prove they can manage everything.
