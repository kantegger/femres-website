The Sex Lives of African Women grows from Nana Darkoa Sekyiamah’s long-running project Adventures from the Bedrooms of African Women. Drawing on conversations with more than thirty African and diasporic women, the book treats sexuality as a field of knowledge rather than a problem to be explained by outsiders. Its speakers discuss heterosexual, queer, polyamorous, monogamous, kinky, celibate, and evolving lives across the continent and diaspora.

The collection’s political force comes from changing the archive. African women’s sexuality is often represented through disease, fertility, violence, or cultural backwardness; pleasure appears either absent or evidence of danger. Sekyiamah does not deny abuse, coercion, religion, or patriarchy. She lets women narrate how those forces coexist with curiosity, fantasy, refusal, self-pleasure, aging, disability, and the difficult work of learning what they want.

Organized around self-discovery, freedom, and healing, the testimonies show liberation as uneven rather than triumphant. Economic independence and supportive communities can widen choice, while stigma, marriage, family obligation, homophobia, and prior trauma continue to shape it. The author’s own reflections prevent her from posing as a neutral collector; she is part of the feminist conversation she records.

Testimonial form also raises questions. A curated chorus cannot represent an entire continent, and the women able or willing to speak about sex may have forms of mobility, language, or security unavailable to others. The book’s diversity is a deliberate counterexample to stereotype, not a demographic survey. Its ethical achievement is to preserve difference without turning disclosure into spectacle.

For readers of feminist sexuality studies, this book shifts attention from whether African women possess agency to the languages and relationships through which agency becomes possible. Pleasure is neither a luxury imported from elsewhere nor automatic proof of freedom. It is a contested capacity to inhabit one’s body, revise inherited scripts, and speak with others beyond shame.
