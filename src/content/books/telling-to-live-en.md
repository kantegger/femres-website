*Telling to Live* emerged from a Latina Feminist Group whose members—poets, oral historians, ethnographers, psychologists, and literary scholars—worked across different racial, national, linguistic, sexual, religious, and class locations. Their testimonios combine life writing, photographs, poetry, letters, and collective reflection to trace migration, family obligation, academic exclusion, sexuality, political awakening, and the costs of becoming professional knowledge producers. The form matters: experience is not inserted as decoration around theory. Lives that universities often divide into private memory and public expertise become the ground from which concepts, methods, and solidarities are made.

Testimonio here is neither unprocessed confession nor a claim that one voice transparently represents a whole community. Contributors address one another, revise the conditions of speaking, and show how narration is shaped by audience, institutional authority, and uneven access to education. The collective refuses a single Latina identity while still asking how common ground can be built. That tension gives the book its feminist methodological importance. Coalition does not require erasing difference, but difference cannot become an excuse for avoiding responsibility. Collaboration itself becomes research: slow, relational labor that challenges the academy’s attachment to individual authorship and detached objectivity.

Because the volume centers scholars and professional storytellers in the United States, it should not be mistaken for a comprehensive map of Latina life or Latin American feminisms. Its institutional location is both a limit and a subject of analysis. For FemRes readers, the book is especially valuable beside women-of-color feminism and decolonial approaches to knowledge production. It demonstrates why “who may know?” cannot be separated from “how must a life be told to become credible?” The answer is not simply to include marginalized stories, but to change the relationships of authority, care, and accountability through which stories become scholarship.
