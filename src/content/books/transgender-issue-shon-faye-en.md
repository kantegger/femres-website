Shon Faye begins *The Transgender Issue* by exposing the title’s trap. British media and politics turn a small, diverse population into a permanent controversy, inviting everyone to debate trans people while material conditions disappear from view. Faye reclaims the phrase to ask what the actual issue is: inadequate healthcare, family rejection, housing insecurity, workplace discrimination, poverty, policing, prisons, and violence. By moving across the life course and through institutions, she replaces abstract arguments about identity with an account of how transphobia organizes survival.

The book’s feminism is deliberately coalitional. Chapters on sex work, incarceration, healthcare, class, and LGBTQ+ politics argue that trans liberation cannot be reduced to respectful language or the success of a visible professional minority. The same systems that punish trans people also distribute precarity through race, disability, migration status, sexuality, and gendered labor. Faye therefore defends solidarity not as a vague appeal to kindness but as a shared project of transforming housing, medicine, work, borders, and punishment. Trans justice can enlarge feminist freedom because it challenges the institutions that decide whose body, family, livelihood, and self-knowledge receive protection.

As a public manifesto, the book synthesizes a wide field and cannot give every community or policy equal depth; its primary frame is contemporary Britain. Readers elsewhere should translate its institutional analysis rather than assume identical laws or services. Its major achievement is rhetorical and political: it refuses the culture war’s agenda without retreating from radical demands. For FemRes, *The Transgender Issue* is a model of accessible structural argument. It asks readers to stop treating trans people as a test of personal tolerance and instead judge a society by whether marginalized people possess the material conditions for ordinary, joyful, self-determined lives.
