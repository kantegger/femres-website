The second edition of *Trans Bodies, Trans Selves* is a large community resource written by and for trans, nonbinary, and gender-expansive people rather than a clinical manual that turns them into objects of expertise. Across more than six hundred pages, contributors address race and culture, immigration, disability, religion, coming out, work, law, health, medical and surgical transition, sexuality, relationships, parenting, childhood, youth, aging, history, arts, and organizing. Its breadth communicates a basic political fact: trans life is a whole life course, not a medical episode or a debate about identity.

The book’s model of knowledge is as important as the information it contains. Multiple voices, quotations, surveys, and community experience prevent any single transition path from becoming the norm. Medical information is placed beside legal rights, employment, kinship, culture, and activism, showing how bodily autonomy depends on institutions and material resources. By helping readers become experts on their own lives, the project challenges the gatekeeping traditions through which clinicians, lawmakers, and families have claimed authority over gender. Royalties and nonprofit outreach also support education and a book-donation program, extending the resource beyond ordinary commercial publishing.

No reference work of this scale can be fully current. Medical protocols, terminology, and law change, and the book remains substantially oriented toward the United States; readers should verify time-sensitive guidance locally. Its encyclopedic form may also conceal disagreement among trans communities if used as a rulebook. Read as a starting point rather than a final authority, however, it is indispensable to FemRes. It joins practical survival knowledge to trans feminism and demonstrates why a serious feminist library needs resources people can use in clinics, workplaces, families, and organizing—not only texts that interpret those institutions from afar.
