Cynthia Enloe’s *Seriously!* asks what becomes visible when women’s experiences and feminist analysis are treated as serious evidence during events officially labeled crises. From financial collapse and revolution to peacekeeping and wartime sexual violence, established experts often declare gender secondary until stability returns. Enloe shows that this postponement is itself political: institutions define a crisis through the knowledge of bankers, diplomats, soldiers, and male opposition leaders while treating domestic workers, wives, women protesters, and survivors as anecdotal.

The chapters practice “feminist curiosity” across apparently separate arenas. A bank bailout depends on ideas about responsible masculinity and feminized service labor; a revolution’s public square rests on food, safety, and care work; peacekeepers’ off-duty conduct affects local economies and sexual politics. Taking these relations seriously changes causal explanation, not merely representation. It reveals who absorbs risk, whose unpaid work makes institutional recovery possible, and which inequalities are rebuilt under the name of returning to normal.

As a collection of interventions, the book offers a method rather than one unified case study, and some examples belong to the political moment around its 2013 publication. Its method remains portable precisely because it resists crisis exceptionalism. FemRes readers can apply its central test to new events: whose account is admitted as expertise, what gendered labor disappears from the timeline, and would the proposed solution still look successful if the lives at the margins were counted from the beginning?
