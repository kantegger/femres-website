*A Gardener in the Wasteland* is not a dutiful illustrated biography of Jotiba Phule. It remakes his 1873 *Gulamgiri* (*Slavery*) as a visual argument. Srividya Natarajan’s narration and Aparajita Ninan’s black-and-white art inherit Phule’s ridicule of Brahman scripture, mythology, and hereditary authority. When hierarchy presents itself as cosmic order, anti-caste critique has to expose the appropriation of labor and monopoly of knowledge beneath sacred tradition.

The narrative places Shudra and “untouchable” oppression in conversation with Atlantic slavery, preserving Phule’s effort to think across global histories while warning against easy equivalence. These systems have distinct legal, racial, and economic histories. Comparison matters not because they are identical, but because liberal nationalism can condemn bondage elsewhere while accepting domination by religion and birth at home.

Weaving Savitribai into the story prevents the “liberating thinker” from remaining exclusively male. Schools, women’s literacy, widowhood, and marital control show caste reproducing itself through gender. Form matters here: exaggeration, collage, and a wasteland resembling a Western strip sacred authority of reverence and make an old argument accessible. They also make visible how comics choose who occupies the frame and who becomes supporting character.

Adaptation necessarily compresses history. Clear heroes and antagonists can reduce complex non-Brahman movements, British colonial contradictions, and the independent thought of Savitribai and other women to Jotiba’s story. The book should not replace *Gulamgiri* or Dalit historiography. It is a provocative route into them, asking why contemporary India must still be taught to see caste.

For FemRes, the work demonstrates that the form of theory’s circulation is political. Anti-caste feminism lives not only in scholarship but in satire, composition, and classroom reading that can dismantle inherited awe. Readers can hold two questions together: how did Phule turn knowledge into resistance, and how do contemporary adapters select, update, and dispute his legacy through visual language?
