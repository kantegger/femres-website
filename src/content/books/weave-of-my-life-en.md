Urmila Pawar's *The Weave of My Life*, translated from Marathi by Maya Pandit, turns autobiography into a history of caste, gender, migration, and political awakening. Its governing image comes from the bamboo baskets woven by Pawar's mother: the act of weaving material for survival becomes linked to the daughter's work of arranging memory into writing. Pawar moves between childhood in the Konkan region, education, marriage, urban employment, literary life, and participation in Ambedkarite and women's movements. Everyday scenes of food, clothing, language, schooling, and domestic conflict show caste not as an abstract ranking but as something learned through the body and enforced in intimate spaces.

The memoir's feminist power lies in its refusal to choose between criticizing Brahmanical caste order and confronting patriarchy within Dalit families and movements. Education and conversion to Buddhism open routes toward dignity, yet women continue to carry domestic labor, sexual respectability, and the emotional costs of male authority. At the same time, mainstream women's organizations can treat caste as secondary and Dalit women as evidence rather than theorists. Pawar answers both exclusions through form: humor, anger, family recollection, movement history, and literary self-making become a Dalit feminist archive in which ordinary women generate knowledge about power.

Autobiography is always selective, and the English translation mediates a text rooted in Marathi speech, regional memory, and political vocabulary. Readers should resist turning Pawar's life into a representative story of all Dalit women or a simple narrative of education overcoming oppression. The book is more demanding than that: individual mobility remains entangled with collective histories, unequal marriages, and institutions that can absorb exceptional subjects without dismantling caste. For FemRes, *The Weave of My Life* demonstrates how life writing can expose the limits of both caste-blind feminism and gender-blind anti-caste politics while preserving contradiction as part of historical truth.
