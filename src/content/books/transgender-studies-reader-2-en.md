Edited by Susan Stryker and Aren Z. Aizura, *The Transgender Studies Reader 2* gathers fifty essays representing the field’s rapid expansion after the first reader. Rather than repeat a historical genealogy, the volume maps emerging work in radical political economy, transfeminism, human-animal relations, temporality, geography, migration, policy, social change, and conflicts within trans communities. Editorial introductions and bibliographic suggestions make the seven-hundred-page collection usable as a teaching archive: readers can see not only established answers but the questions through which “transgender studies 2.0” was redefining its object.

The shift is politically important. Trans phenomena are not confined to identity, medicine, or a Euro-American narrative of individual transition; they move through racial capitalism, disability, borders, labor, coloniality, space, media, and more-than-human life. Essays also preserve disagreement over categories, methods, and coalition rather than presenting trans studies as a unified doctrine. This makes the reader valuable to feminism: it demonstrates how trans scholarship can challenge universal claims about “women,” embodiment, reproduction, and liberation while also being transformed by women-of-color, postcolonial, disability, and materialist critique.

No anthology can make a field’s expansion comprehensive, and the 2013 publication date means newer work on platform politics, contemporary backlash, abolition, and global trans movements must be sought elsewhere. Its size and academic language can also make it a demanding entry point. Used selectively, however, the reader is a powerful research infrastructure. For FemRes, it is best approached as a map of debates rather than a single argument, paired with the first volume to see how a field moves from recovering suppressed histories toward rethinking political economy, geography, and the boundaries of the human itself.
