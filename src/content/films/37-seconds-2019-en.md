The protagonist of *37 Seconds*, Yuma Takada, is a young manga artist with cerebral palsy. She ghosts artwork for a glamorous cousin who takes public credit; her mother organizes her time and body in the name of protection; and an editor tells her she lacks the sexual experience needed to create adult manga. The film connects workplace exploitation, family care, and sexual autonomy. Yuma is fighting not merely to leave home but to be recognized as an adult capable of creating, desiring, taking risks, and making mistakes.

Its major intervention is to refuse the two familiar frames of the disabled woman as asexual innocence or inspirational overcoming. Yuma’s exploration of sex includes awkwardness and risk as well as curiosity, pleasure, transaction, and learning boundaries. The camera neither medicalizes her body nor makes sexual experience a miraculous cure. Her agency does not depend on becoming more able-bodied; it grows through access to information, mobility, and authority over relationships.

The mother-daughter relationship is more complex than a story of one controlling parent. Her mother’s care comes from real love and long labor, but it is shaped by a system that provides little public support. Because society makes her nearly irreplaceable, necessity can become possession. Yuma’s independence does not deny dependency; it demands that dependency not equal ownership. This is a central disability-justice distinction: autonomy means having power over what assistance is needed, who provides it, and where one’s life goes.

Yuma enters Tokyo nightlife, sexual services, and new friendship, meeting people who do not first approach her as a burden. These sequences broaden the social spaces in which disabled people may appear, but they merit a critical reading: sex workers sometimes carry the narrative task of advancing the protagonist, while their working conditions remain comparatively thin. Even so, the film takes communication, consent, and assistance seriously as components of intimacy rather than excluding disabled bodies from desire.

*37 Seconds* won both the Panorama Audience Award and the CICAE Art Cinema Award at the 2019 Berlinale. Its feminist value lies beyond positive representation. Yuma is not required to be a morally flawless spokesperson. She withholds, acts impulsively, hurts others, and revises her understanding of family history. A disabled woman becomes a character rather than a symbol, and freedom means neither escaping her body nor eliminating reliance on others, but gaining greater negotiating power over authorship, care, sexuality, and the future.
