*Lingua Franca* follows Olivia, an undocumented Filipina trans woman living in Brooklyn, who works as an elderly-care provider while trying to stay visible enough to work but invisible enough to avoid immigration enforcement. The film’s strongest move is to frame her life as a chain of ordinary acts—cooking, cleaning, helping with medication, maintaining routines—rather than a sequence of melodramatic incidents. That insistence on the everyday turns the film into an anatomy of precarity: we see how a legal status can be enforced not only by police, but by the social obligation to care in environments that require trust, emotional proximity, and trustworthiness it does not always guarantee.

The film also refuses the usual “rescue” grammar of social dramas. Olivia’s body is neither presented as spectacle nor symbol, but as labor under constraint: a body carrying both care expertise and the constant risk of becoming legible as a threat. Sandoval’s direction keeps this tension in focus through restrained camera movement, controlled color, and ellipses that leave space for structural interpretation. We are not encouraged to pity Olivia as an individual exception; we are asked to recognize the policy architecture around her.

Within a feminist lens, *Lingua Franca* is a study of relational power. Immigration policing is shown not only through legal institutions but through intimate arrangements—the home as workplace, romance as potential pathway, friendship as fragile support under surveillance. The film argues that care economies are never neutral; they reproduce gendered vulnerability, racial hierarchy, and class dependence at the same time.

After its Venice Days premiere in 2019, the film was acquired by Ava DuVernay’s ARRAY and later released on Netflix in 2020. Its visibility in independent circuits came with recognition including the Independent Spirit John Cassavetes Award and GLAAD consideration, but its political force is less in awards than in the way it makes us read care as a governance regime, not a private virtue.
